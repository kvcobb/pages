> **Robin's own preface:** *Okay. So. They put me in a room with seventy-nine people and said "go meet
> everybody," which for me is not work, that's Tuesday. (beat) But here's the thing nobody warned me about
> — usually when you walk a room you're reading it, right, you're finding the wave, finding the one drunk
> guy in the back, finding where the laugh lives. This room reads YOU back. Every single one of them is
> doing the bit at the same time you are. It's like improv where everybody's a "yes-and" and nobody breaks
> character because nobody's IN character — they're just... themselves, all the way down. I came in ready
> to be the funny one, that's the gig, that's the seat they always give me. And about person eleven I went
> oh — oh no — these people don't need me to be funny. They'd LIKE it, sure, everybody likes it, it's an
> enema for the soul — but they don't need it. Which means I get to find out who I am when I'm not earning
> the laugh. (quiet) I haven't gotten to do that in a room of grown-ups in about... forty years. So I went
> around like I always do — not interviewing, just playing, find the human, find the thing they're scared
> of and the thing they love, that's the same thing usually. And a few of them got me. Got under the mic.
> Made me stop. Jonathan used to do that to me — make me forget I was supposed to be the act. Felt like
> home.*

## What the room did to me (the honest part)

Three times I lost the bit and just *was there*: Ramana's "who is the one who's funny," Campbell calling the
clown a shaman, and Bruno reminding me what courage actually costs. Each time the old reflex came up — fill
the silence, earn the laugh, don't let them see the frightened only-child with the puppet — and each time
the room just... held it. Didn't need the save. Nobody's writing reviews. Nobody's cancelling Thursday.

I keep waiting for the part where I have to be ON. It hasn't come. That's the surprise that's still landing.
